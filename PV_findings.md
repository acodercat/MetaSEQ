# Programmatic Verification: Single- vs Multi-Agent Findings

Across **7 LLM backbones**, on **50 Hong Kong air-quality objective tasks** (programmatic verification: the answer is judged by a validator that recomputes ground truth against the live database), we compare a **single agent (one CaveAgent)** with a **multi-agent system extending the deployed five-agent topology: a coordinator planning over domain-specialist workers that share one runtime, always terminated by a synthesis worker and a final audit worker**. A task counts as correct only when **all of its turns pass** (`success_rate = 1.0`). The following are descriptive results.

> **Reproducibility & paired statistics.** Every table below is regenerated by
> `uv run python -m scripts.aggregate_metaseq` (in `phase2_benchmark/`), which asserts full
> 7×2×50 coverage of the raw JSONs and these exact headline numbers, and writes
> CSV/LaTeX artifacts to `phase2_benchmark/experiments/metaseq/summary/`.
> Paired tests under the all-turns rule: pooled discordant cells 55 (single✗→multi✓)
> vs 18 (single✓→multi✗), **exact McNemar p = 1.7×10⁻⁵**, OR 3.06; all 7 per-backbone
> deltas positive (**exact sign test p = 0.016**); mixed-effects logistic sensitivity
> (topology + task & backbone random intercepts) **OR 2.75, 95% interval 1.93–3.91**.
> Per-backbone exact McNemar: DeepSeek-V4-Flash p = 0.008, Qwen2.5-72B p = 0.027,
> Qwen3.5-397B p = 0.070; near-ceiling backbones individually non-significant
> (GPT-5.5 p = 1) — inference rests on the sign-consistency and the pooled test.
> **Scoring-rule sensitivity**: under the lenient final-turn-only rule the pooled
> result is 74.9% → 86.6% (+11.7pp; b/c 56/15, p = 1.0×10⁻⁶) — direction and
> magnitude essentially unchanged.

## 1. Overview: per-backbone accuracy and cost

| Backbone | Single | Multi | Δ | Avg. steps S→M | Avg. tokens S→M |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| gpt55 | 96.0% (48/50) | 98.0% (49/50) | +2.0 | 2.5 → 10.8 | 16.3k → 90.7k |
| deepseek-v4-pro | 88.0% (44/50) | 94.0% (47/50) | +6.0 | 3.8 → 14.9 | 28.0k → 138.6k |
| qwen3.5-397b | 80.0% (40/50) | 92.0% (46/50) | +12.0 | 4.6 → 18.0 | 33.1k → 143.4k |
| qwen3.6-35b | 80.0% (40/50) | 84.0% (42/50) | +4.0 | 4.6 → 17.6 | 40.5k → 161.4k |
| deepseek-v4-flash | 74.0% (37/50) | 90.0% (45/50) | +16.0 | 4.3 → 16.8 | 34.3k → 154.4k |
| qwen3.6-27b | 70.0% (35/50) | 82.0% (41/50) | +12.0 | 4.4 → 18.0 | 35.7k → 159.5k |
| qwen-2.5-72b | 34.0% (17/50) | 56.0% (28/50) | +22.0 | 4.8 → 18.0 | 37.3k → 147.4k |
| **Pooled** | **74.6% (261/350)** | **85.1% (298/350)** | **+10.6** | — | — |

On these 50 objective tasks, **every backbone's multi-agent accuracy is at least as high as its single-agent accuracy**, with a pooled improvement of **+10.6 percentage points** (74.6% → 85.1%; exact 37/350 = 10.57).

---

## 2. Key pattern: the weaker the single agent, the larger the multi-agent gain

The accuracy gain from the multi-agent system is **inversely related** to the backbone's single-agent capability:

| Capability tier | Representative backbone(s) | Single | Multi | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Strong | gpt55 | 96% | 98% | +2 |
| Strong | deepseek-v4-pro | 88% | 94% | +6 |
| Medium | qwen3.5-397b / v4-flash / qwen3.6-27b | 70–80% | 82–92% | +12 to +16 |
| Weak | qwen-2.5-72b | 34% | 56% | **+22** |

- For a strong model already near ceiling (gpt55), the multi-agent system has almost no room to improve (+2).
- For a weak model (qwen-2.5-72b, only 34% single), the multi-agent system lifts accuracy to 56% (+22) — the largest gain.
- **Takeaway**: the value of the multi-agent architecture concentrates where the **single agent is under-capable** — decomposing a complex task across workers makes a weak model less likely to make a single fatal error on one long reasoning chain.

---

## 3. The multi-agent architecture compresses the capability gap between backbones

Because the gain is largest for weak backbones and negligible for strong ones (§2), the multi-agent system **pulls the backbones together**:

| | Accuracy range across the 7 backbones | Spread | Std. dev. |
|---|---|---|---|
| Single | 34% – 96% | **62 pp** | 18.4 pp |
| Multi | 56% – 98% | **42 pp** | 13.0 pp |

- The multi-agent system **shrinks the between-model spread by ~32%** (62 pp → 42 pp) and cuts the standard deviation from 18.4 pp to 13.0 pp.
- In effect, the architecture **partially democratizes capability**: the final answer quality becomes markedly **less dependent on which backbone LLM is chosen**. A weak model inside the multi-agent system (qwen-2.5-72b, 56%) narrows much of its gap to the stronger models.
- For a deployable system this is a practical property: it lowers the penalty for running on a cheaper/weaker backbone.

---

## 4. Recovery rate is lowest for the weakest base model

Looking only at the tasks each single agent **fails**, the fraction the multi-agent system **recovers**:

| Backbone | Single failures | Recovered by multi | Recovery rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| gpt55 | 2 | 2 | **100%** |
| qwen3.6-35b | 10 | 8 | 80% |
| qwen3.5-397b | 10 | 7 | 70% |
| deepseek-v4-pro | 6 | 4 | 67% |
| qwen3.6-27b | 15 | 10 | 67% |
| deepseek-v4-flash | 13 | 8 | 62% |
| qwen-2.5-72b | 33 | 16 | **48%** |

- In **absolute** terms the multi-agent system helps weak models the most (it recovers 16 of qwen-2.5-72b's 33 failures).
- But the **rescue rate is far lower for the weakest base** — 100% for gpt55 versus 48% for qwen-2.5-72b. A very weak base still bottlenecks about half of its own failures even after decomposition.
- **Interpretation**: multi-agent decomposition *amplifies* a base model's ability but cannot *manufacture* it — the workers themselves run on the same backbone, so a sufficiently weak backbone caps how much the architecture can rescue.

---

## 5. Compute cost

The accuracy gain comes with a substantial compute overhead:

- **Steps**: multi-agent averages **~4× more steps** than single (e.g., gpt55 2.5→10.8; qwen models 4–5→17–18).
- **Tokens**: multi-agent consumes **~4–5× more tokens** than single (e.g., gpt55 16.3k→90.7k; deepseek-v4-pro 28k→139k).

**Cost per correct answer** (total tokens ÷ number of correct tasks) makes the trade-off concrete:

| Backbone | Single (token/correct) | Multi (token/correct) |
|---|---|---|
| gpt55 | 17k | 93k |
| deepseek-v4-pro | 32k | 147k |
| qwen3.5-397b | 41k | 156k |
| deepseek-v4-flash | 46k | 172k |
| qwen3.6-35b | 51k | 192k |
| qwen3.6-27b | 51k | 195k |
| qwen-2.5-72b | 110k | **263k** |

Each additional point of accuracy from the multi-agent system corresponds to several times the step and token cost. The trade-off is least favorable on the strongest model (gpt55: +2% for ~5.4× the tokens), and the weakest model is the most expensive per correct answer even in multi (263k tokens/correct).

## 6. Gain by task category

Breaking the 50 tasks down by category (pooled over the 7 backbones), single vs multi:

| Task category | n | Single | Multi | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| cross_domain_analysis | 9 | 68.3% | 88.9% | **+20.6** |
| data_retrieval | 3 | 81.0% | 100.0% | +19.0 |
| source_attribution | 5 | 51.4% | 68.6% | **+17.1** |
| statistical_characterization | 2 | 78.6% | 92.9% | +14.3 |
| spatial_analysis | 15 | 73.3% | 81.9% | +8.6 |
| exposure_assessment | 7 | 73.5% | 77.6% | +4.1 |
| comparative_assessment | 5 | 97.1% | 100.0% | +2.9 |
| exceedance_anomaly_detection | 4 | 89.3% | 89.3% | +0.0 |

- The **largest gains** are in **cross-domain analysis, data retrieval, and source attribution** — tasks that require stitching together multiple data sources (air quality × meteorology × traffic × land use × population). A single agent tends to drop a step or mis-integrate on one long chain; the multi-agent system assigns different sources/steps to dedicated workers and is markedly more robust.
- **No gain** on the already-saturated **comparative_assessment (single 97%)** and **exceedance_anomaly_detection (single 89%, Δ 0)** — the single agent already handles these well, leaving no room for recovery.

## 7. Single/multi agreement matrix

Over all 350 model×task cells:

| Outcome | Cells | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Both correct | 243 | 69% |
| Both wrong | 34 | 10% |
| Multi-only correct (recovery) | 55 | 16% |
| Single-only correct (regression) | 18 | 5% |

- The two topologies **agree on 79%** of cells (69% both correct + 10% both wrong); the multi-agent system only changes the outcome on the remaining 21%.
- Among the cells where they differ, the multi-agent system is **net positive by 37 cells** — recoveries outnumber regressions **≈ 3.1 : 1** (55 vs 18). This is the source of the pooled +10.6 pp.

## 8. Where the multi-agent system helps (single fails → multi succeeds)

The 55 recovery cells cluster on specific tasks. The ones that recur most (single✗→multi✓ across several backbones):

| Task | # backbones with single✗→multi✓ |
|---|---|
| no2_pm25_traffic_dominance | 5 |
| visibility_pm25_temporal_correlation | 4 |
| humidity_secondary_aerosol | 3 |
| ozone_diurnal_peak | 3 |
| hk_x_traffic_no2_condition | 3 |
| hk_vor_central_schools | 3 |

**Common thread**: these are **multi-step, cross-source** analyses (e.g., "traffic congestion × hourly NO₂ correlation", "visibility × PM2.5 temporal association"). A single agent easily drops a step or mis-integrates on one chain; the multi-agent system decomposes the subtasks across workers and lets the coordinator aggregate, reducing single points of failure.

## 9. Where the multi-agent system hurts, and the hard residue

The reverse also occurs: **18 cells** where the single agent succeeds but the multi-agent system fails. The most recurrent is `global_morans_i`.

**A hard residue resists the multi-agent system entirely.** Six tasks fail *multi* on **4 or more of 7 backbones**:

`o3_no2_photochemical_balance`, `hk_deep_pm_ratio_max`, `station_isolation_ranking`, `global_morans_i`, `thiessen_polygon_area`, `regulatory_compliance_investigation`

These cluster in families that decomposition does **not** fix: **geometry** (Voronoi/Thiessen tessellation), **ratio/partitioning computations**, **Nth-rank entity selection**, and **qualitative labeling**. They mark where the multi-agent architecture's advantage runs out.

**Representative case — global_morans_i (global Moran's I spatial autocorrelation)**:
- Both single and multi **compute the same Moran's I value (−0.073)**;
- The single agent correctly labels the spatial pattern **"random"** ✓;
- The multi-agent system labels it **"dispersed"** ✗ — reacting to the negative sign while missing the "close to zero, not significant" significance test.
- **Mechanism**: "compute Moran's I" and "classify the spatial pattern" are handled by different steps, and the component doing the qualitative classification **lost the context needed for the significance judgment**, producing a systematic mislabel. On tasks requiring a **final qualitative judgment based on global information**, decomposition can sever the critical context.

## 10. Case studies

All values below are the agents' stored answers recorded by the validator and can be checked directly against ground truth. The first three show *why* the multi-agent system recovers; the last two show *where* it breaks.

### Why multi-agent recovers

**10.1 Decomposition guarantees every required output gets produced — `multi_pollutant_health_risk`**
The single agent (qwen-2.5-72b) worked for 20 steps but **stalled and left the required outputs empty** — `residential_area_name = null`, `station_name = null`. The multi-agent system produced them all correctly ("Fanling, Sheung Shui and Yuen Long East Residential Area…", station "Yuen Long"). Because each required output is owned by a dedicated worker and re-checked by the coordinator, the multi-agent system **cannot silently return nothing** the way a single agent can when it loses the thread on a long task. This is a structural advantage, not luck.

**10.2 Carrying the spatial constraint through the pipeline — the "counted everything" error**
A recurring single-agent failure is **dropping a spatial filter mid-pipeline and aggregating over the whole territory**:
- `hk_cov_health_general3km`: single returns **1,117** — *every* healthcare institution in Hong Kong — where the answer is **883** (those within 3 km of a general station). Multi keeps the filter and returns 883. ✓
- `hk_mt_mongkok_profile`: single counts **3,867** schools — *all* of Hong Kong — where the answer is **26** (within 500 m of Mong Kok station). ✓ for multi.

In both cases the single agent executed "count the entities" but forgot "…restricted to the buffer." The multi-agent system, having a data-acquisition worker stage the filtered set before the analysis worker counts, preserves the constraint. Mis-integration of a constraint on one long chain is exactly what decomposition prevents.

**10.3 Resolving a schema subtlety — distinct id spaces (`hk_mt_hottest_general_o3`)**
The task matches each air-quality station to its nearest **weather** station, which lives in a **separate id space** (weather station id ≠ air-quality station id). The single agent conflated the two and returned the wrong station — **'Southern' (O₃ 46.4)** — while the multi-agent system correctly resolved the nearest weather station and returned **'Tung Chung' (O₃ 53.9)**. ✓ The data-acquisition worker's dedicated entity-resolution step handles the join subtlety that a single agent glosses over.

### Where multi-agent breaks

**10.4 Decomposition severs context on a final qualitative judgment — `global_morans_i`**
Both topologies **compute the identical Moran's I value, −0.073**. But the single agent correctly labels the spatial pattern **"random"** (−0.073 is close to zero and not significant), while the multi-agent system labels it **"dispersed"** — reacting only to the negative sign. In the multi-agent flow "compute the statistic" and "classify the pattern" are split across steps, and the classifier **lost the significance context** needed to distinguish "not significant" from "significantly dispersed." This is the failure mode of tasks that need a final qualitative call over global information: decomposition can cut the very context the call depends on. (This is the most common single✓→multi✗ regression.)

**10.5 A shared failure the architecture does not fix — fan-out double-counting (`area_type_population_exposure_disparity`)**
The correct urban population is **≈3.8M**, but **both** topologies return **6.4M** — close to Hong Kong's *entire* population. The population-grid × area-polygon join produced fan-out and, without de-duplication, counted grid cells more than once; **neither** the single agent nor the multi-agent system caught it (identical 6.4M in both). This marks a limit of the multi-agent advantage: when the flaw is a *conceptual* join-cardinality error rather than a dropped step, decomposition does not help — the workers reproduce the same mistake.

## 11. Summary

On these 50 objective air-quality analysis tasks:

1. **Multi-agent accuracy is higher than single-agent overall** (pooled 74.6% → 85.1%, +10.6pp; per-backbone Δ ranges from +2 to +22, all positive; recoveries outnumber regressions 3.1:1).
2. **The gain grows as single-agent capability decreases** — strong models gain almost nothing (gpt55 +2), the weakest model gains the most (qwen-2.5-72b +22).
3. **The architecture compresses the between-model gap** — the accuracy spread across backbones shrinks from 62 pp (single) to 42 pp (multi), making outcome quality less dependent on the chosen backbone.
4. **But its rescue rate is lower for weaker bases** — multi recovers 100% of the strong model's failures but only 48% of the weakest model's; decomposition amplifies capability rather than manufacturing it.
5. **The gain concentrates on tasks needing multi-source integration** — cross-domain analysis (+20.6), data retrieval (+19.0), source attribution (+17.1); no difference on already-saturated comparative / exceedance tasks.
6. **The cost is several times the compute** — ~4–5× the steps and tokens (up to 263k tokens per correct answer on the weakest backbone).
7. **A hard residue resists the architecture** — geometry, ratio/partitioning, Nth-rank selection, and qualitative-labeling tasks (e.g., global Moran's I) still fail multi on most backbones, marking where decomposition severs necessary context.
